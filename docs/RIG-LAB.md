# Cairn Hound — articulated asset laboratory

This is an original Blender technical study under `assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/`. It does **not** replace the illustrated runtime creature or establish production art quality. Blender 4.3.2 was actually available and used for geometry, skinning, animation, shader baking, glTF export, and rendered inspection. No paid/external assets were imported. The reference was the top-left Cairn Hound illustration in `public/art/companions-atlas.png`, inspected for tone and anatomical intent; the reference pixels were not edited or converted into geometry.

The latest owner direction favors painted 2.5D sprites and depth effects. Heavy Blender work is paused; the laboratory is preserved for later investigation. Current follow-up research is recorded in `assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/PAINTED-DEPTH-NOTES.md`. No forced 3D runtime promotion is proposed.

## What exists

- Original continuous lofted torso/neck, elongated skull and hinged jaw, four digitigrade limbs with articulated paws/digits, spine/tail, torn ears, exposed mineralized ribs, dorsal armor, small ember eyes, and geometric mane tufts.
- A 28-bone armature with vertex-group weights. Every vertex is weighted; measured total weights range from 0.99999988 to 1.00000012. Torso/joint weights blend bones; teeth and armor are mostly rigidly weighted components.
- Five named actions at 24 fps: `Idle_BreathAndWatch` (frames 1–49), `Anticipation_Crouch` (1–13), `Lunge_CommandStrike` (1–25), `Hit_Recoil` (1–17), and `Death_Collapse` (1–41). These are approximate posed actions, not finished animal locomotion or contact-constrained animation.
- Saved `.blend` and `.glb`, actual hero/profile/rear/lunge/death renders, and twelve orbital turntable images plus an encoded turntable video. The original model has 25,065 Blender vertices, 49,328 triangles, and seven material slots. glTF splits vertices along boundaries: the unbaked variant has 27,040 position entries; adding UV seams produces 33,398 in the PBR variant. A separate budget experiment preserves these originals and measures a lower-triangle candidate.
- A separate portable PBR variant with automatic UV unwrap and Blender-baked 2048×2048 basecolor and tangent-normal maps. These are renders of the procedural model's shaders, not edits of the reference image. Roughness/metallic values remain per-material factors.

The main geometry/action generator is `scripts/blender_hound_lab.py`; a copy is retained with the assets. `bake_pbr.py` creates the portable-material variant. `verify_gltf.py` independently imports the exported file into a new Blender scene, checks bones/actions/weights, measures posed geometry, and renders the imported asset. These are technical checks, not independent agent acceptance or a human enjoyment test.

## Iterations and observed defects

The first inspected render read as an angular mannequin with uniform bright spikes and smooth toy-like hide. It failed the game's realistic-creature art direction. The first-pass script, geometry, export, and available renders are retained under `first-pass/`.

A second pass added subdivision, irregular bone ridges, an original skull carapace, procedural material variation, scars, and geometric mane tufts. Subdivision shrank the terminal limb surfaces and exposed gaps above the paws. The inspected second-pass files are retained under `second-pass/`. The next pass added weighted ankle/pad junction geometry, improving those visible gaps. These changes do not make the anatomy production quality.

Export inspection found a concrete material defect: the unbaked ColorRamp shader links produced glTF materials without a basecolor texture/factor, so a conforming importer fell back to white. The original export is retained as `unbaked-export.glb`. The PBR variant bakes basecolor and normal maps, embeds them in `cairn-hound-lab-pbr.glb`, and retains the separate `.png` inputs and `.blend`. Actual before/after imported renders and reports are generated by the verification script; review those files before treating the material repair as accepted.

Actual before/after imported images were inspected: the initial export rendered white, while the PBR variant restored dark hide, bone armor, eye, scars, and jaw colors. Import retained 28 bones, all five clips, and weighted vertices. GLTF-INVENTORY.json records two embedded images and texture references on all seven materials in the PBR variant. This establishes the narrow material-portability repair, not final art quality.

Animation inspection found that death moved the creature completely beneath the floor: minimum geometry Z was -1.2995 metres. Per-frame inspection also found smaller penetrations in anticipation, lunge, hit, and idle. `repair_contacts.py` creates a separate contact variant by projecting the whole actor above the plane and recentering its death pose. The sampled minimum Z becomes approximately 0.01m for every action. This is not planted-foot IK and can cancel crouch depth or leave stiff support. The resulting death still looked unnatural on inspection, despite being visible above the floor.

Rendered lunge also revealed a wrong jaw hinge axis: rotating the assumed local axis twisted the jaw rather than opening it. `repair_articulation.py` maps an anatomical sagittal axis into each bone's rest space, repairs jaw opening, folds death limbs, and rechecks ground clearance. The inspected lunge now visibly opens downward at the hinge. Folded death limbs still do not establish physically credible falling/contact. All earlier variants and rejected renders remain available.

This system Blender build has no OpenImageDenoise support. The initial denoised render failed; actual successful renders use Cycles CPU with denoising disabled. Optional Draco compression is unavailable; exports are uncompressed. Neither limitation prevents ordinary glTF export.

## Current art assessment

**Reject runtime promotion at the requested craft bar.** This is the asset author's assessment, not a fabricated independent consensus. The asset demonstrates an articulated pipeline but remains a stylized procedural assemblage. Smooth simplified head/body masses, an overly regular armor rhythm, coarse fur substitute, rounded paw junctions, and limited anatomical surface integration still read below the detailed grim illustration. Its nearly 50,000 triangles are expensive relative to the visible detail. A dark material alone does not solve that gap.

No inverse kinematics or contact constraints exist. Limb pivots, root movement, floor contact, jaw motion, and skin deformation need actual motion review. Procedural parts overlap rather than forming a retopologized organic surface. Automatic UV islands need seam and texel-density review. There are no LODs, facial shapes, authored gait, production groom, or engine integration. The PBR repair addresses portability, not sculpt quality.

The next useful work is to test the imported animated asset and fix measured floor/deformation defects, then improve integrated anatomy, surface detail, fur silhouette, UVs, and geometry efficiency. Obtain an independent visual/animation review before any runtime replacement. The current illustrated arena remains the safer presentation baseline while this lab develops.

The current budget experiment uses rest-pose collapse decimation before the armature modifier, preserving actions/material maps. Its report and actual hero render must be compared with the preserved higher-triangle source before accepting any reduction. It is neither authored retopology nor an integrated runtime LOD system.

The completed budget experiment measures 49,328→22,196 triangles and retains weighted vertices, 28 bones, and five clips. The hero view preserves the broad silhouette on author inspection, but other-angle deformation/seams remain unverified. Independent visual review in `reviews/visual-v0.3.md` confirms the narrow PBR repair and rejects runtime replacement for toy anatomy, regular armor, detached paws, and floating/stiff death support. That review supersedes any implied artistic acceptance from these technical metrics.

`plant_feet_ik.py` adds four nondeforming controls (32 total bones, 28 deforming), two-segment IK, and world-oriented pads. The first overconstrained attempt missed targets by up to ~0.10m and is preserved in `ik-first-pass/`. Allowing ball-like upper joints, keeping lower-joint hinges, and removing conflicting pose keys reduces sampled anticipation/hit/lunge IK target error to about 0.00000065m; the idle maximum remains ~0.0046m and fore-digit movement about 0.001m. Hind digits remain planted through the lunge while front targets deliberately advance/lift. This is author-generated Blender evidence; imported baked-IK checks and temporal quality review are pending the heavy-work pause. It does not repair the rejected anatomy or turn the prior death into credible mass/contact.

## Reproduction and promotion gate

From the repository root:

```sh
blender --background --python scripts/blender_hound_lab.py
blender --background --python assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/bake_pbr.py
blender --background --python assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/repair_contacts.py
blender --background --python assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/repair_articulation.py
blender --background --python assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/verify_gltf.py -- cairn-hound-lab-articulation.glb gltf-articulation
blender --background --python assets/rig-lab/hound-v0.3/optimize_mesh.py
```

Run variants into preserved named folders before another destructive rebuild; existing first/second-pass inputs and historical runtime versions must remain intact. REPORT.json records geometry/action counts; PBR-REPORT.json records baked maps; imported verification reports and logs record actual checks. Final hashes must be refreshed after the renderer closes its log and after turntable video encoding, rather than hashing an actively changing log.

Before promotion: independent reference comparison at battle scale and close range, credible grim anatomy/silhouette/materials, observed command/impact/death timing, contact/deformation checks, matching imported materials, justified mesh/texture budgets and LODs, provenance/rights review, runtime performance, and a reversible comparison against current art. A working rig or exported GLB is a checkpoint, not an acceptable replacement by itself.
