# Lanternbound engine decisions — v0.1.0

The reducer follows ENGINE-CONTRACT.md and has no renderer, timers, I/O, ambient randomness or mutation of incoming game states. xorshift32 seed/rng state and incrementing unit ids make action sequences reproducible after JSON saves. Illegal actions return the original object.

The ten-node trail offers battle/event, camp/shop and elite/battle branches, ending in the Hollow Crown. Deck and hunter HP persist between nodes. Companions are rebuilt each battle so temporary rally bonuses, shields and readied commands reset. Each companion owns one real card copy: its card stays out of all piles until death, then returns to shared discard. Card-id multiplicity is conserved and checked by save validation. Drawing stops at twelve cards. Firefly Reading costs one energy to prevent free draw loops. A resolving spell enters discard after its effect, so it cannot draw itself.

Every living companion may receive one manual command per turn, including its summon turn. Second Wind permits an additional command. Max six companions. Moth spell shields, rally attack buffs, pack-scaled Briar Lance, communion recovery and summon-trigger buffs permit distinct collection plans. Camp training permanently enhances the first unenhanced summon, then a spell; this choice is deterministic because the contract's camp action has no card target. Rest restores 18 HP up to maximum. Normal and elite wins restore 3/5 HP and grant 25/40 gold. Elite wins award an unowned relic. Reward and shop inventory have unique card ids; reward is optional. Cards cost 55 gold for summons or 40 for spells; removal costs 35 and preserves at least five cards.

Intents are computed only at player-turn start and remain stable during player actions. An imp chooses the weakest companion, a wolf the front companion; absent companions yield hunter targeting. If a targeted companion dies before an enemy resolves, its announced intent falls back to the hunter, never another companion. Wisp damage ignores block; its visible label explicitly states this. Sentinel guards alternate with heavy attacks. Witch damage alternates. Crown guards, quakes all targets, then strikes the hunter. Armor gained by an enemy remains until the player has one turn to respond, then expires before the next enemy actions. All enemies execute their announced intents in roster order, removing dead companions between actions. Hunter death resolves before victory, including self-damage from Kindle. Block lasts only until the next player turn.

Difficulty is 0–2: additional enemy HP is +2 per level and attack scales with node progression; Crown quake is +1 damage per difficulty level. Story difficulty is intentionally forgiving for deliberate play. Automated win rates describe policy strength and balance, never establish subjective enjoyment or Steam readiness.

`validateState` checks bounded numeric/string/array fields, recognized definitions with own-property checks, phase-route consistency, unit uid uniqueness and allocation, intent target syntax, card conservation, terminal states and card/relic identities. Arbitrary objects are handled in a try/catch so corrupt saves produce false. Menu is presentation-only and cannot be restored as a saved run. An active encounter must match a choice offered at its actual node; node ten permits only boss combat, preventing a forged final battle from creating an unreachable reward state. It is a local-save structural/invariant check, not a cryptographic anti-cheat mechanism.

Verification: `npx tsx --test tests/engine.test.ts` exercises meaningful conservation, purity, deterministic serialized runs, invalid actions, targeting/fallback, armor, distinct enemy cycles, synergies, rewards/economy, training/recovery and terminal behavior; a seeded legal-random run test checks each intermediate save. `npx tsx scripts/simulate.ts 100` compares seeded legal-random and fixed heuristic policies and reports incomplete runs explicitly. Independent review and human playtesting remain required to evaluate clarity, strategy and enjoyment.
