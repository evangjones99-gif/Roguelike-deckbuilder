Art decision: choose PAINTED DEPTH as the primary direction to develop, with deliberate silhouette/edge cleanup. Do not adopt either panel as final game artwork. The left better supports the owner's gritty, realistic-looking monster-hunter brief: cold moonlight, warm iron/fire accents, weathered material, wet ground contact and volumetric creatures. Its dangerous hound, broad spider, heavy giant and translucent revenant have useful category differences. Light and reflected contact help them inhabit one environment. These qualities address disconnection more directly than adding a generic saturated frame.

The left is also too busy for small combat cells: repeated skull texture, sharp rubble, orange cracks and multiple bright points compete; the foreground hound/spider limbs merge with floor and cards. The towering giant and partially cropped edge ghost work as a poster encounter, not proof of six distinguishable legal targets. Prioritize one clear contour per creature, readable head/weapon/contact foot, lower background contrast immediately behind it, and restrained emissive accents. Keep an original charcoal/slate/bone palette with small rust/ember accents and cold spectral light, maintaining distinct warm/cold faction signals without relying on color alone. Separate shapes and value groups before adding skull/jewelry detail. This is a proposed direction, not extracted colorimetry or asset acceptance.

The SHARP DARK FANTASY panel has stronger graphic edge control and flatter, clearer chunks, particularly the hound outline and giant's silhouette. Those shapes are easier to separate at low resolutions and to plan as layered joints. It supplies a useful readability discipline. Its broad black flats, hard outlines and busy antlers read more like a dramatic inked poster and lose some convincing light/material volume. Making it the sole style would move away from the current realistic-looking brief. Borrowing clean value groups and clear contours into a painted target is a concrete production adjustment, not a mandate to combine every effect from both panels.

Practical asset pipeline
Start with one original encounter of the same hunter/hound/spider/revenant/large quarry identities, with fixed camera, measured feet and silhouette scale. Develop a clean painted creature sheet with authored anticipation, attack/contact, recoil and recovery/death silhouettes; preserve stable full-cell scale/anchors and distinct poses rather than attempting to animate a poster by sliding it. Layered body/limb cuts and a few hand-painted pose changes can support2.5D lighting/contact and interpolated transitions without a full3D dependency. Dense mobile readability must be judged at the actual64–112px body bands and real background values, including reduced motion. Fog/glow/reflection belong mainly in the scene/effects layer so a standalone sprite keeps readable edges and transparent contact anatomy. Do not paint UI badges or cards into the battle backdrop. Rules, costs, targets and readable consequences remain independent live UI.
