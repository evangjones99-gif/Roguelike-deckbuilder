## What earns acceptance, and what remains weak

In the 1920×720 twelve-actor natural sequence, the mouth closes while the painted upper muzzle, skull, extended paws and body remain recognizably the same creature. Reverse-facing equal-age contact shows the same local movement towards the other side of the field. It reads as a bite rather than a whole-head squash. At 390px the lower-jaw movement is only a few pixels and competes with impact effects and the much larger leap; it is incremental polish, not a compelling mobile showcase or a solution to small monster readability.

I individually viewed 39 own production captures and one own renderer-only opacity/anatomy image, plus four exact author normal-size contact PNGs referenced through their frozen packet. Own production captures include the full wide/mobile HTML and complete battlefield sequences, both-facing equal-age contact, recovery, death, reduced motion and live resizing. `VIEWED-CAPTURES.json` explicitly identifies those inspected files; it does not claim all 59 captured JPEGs were viewed. Their four sampled wall ages begin 207.8–209.8ms after the canonical save mark and include contact/recovery observations; these are observed wall-age frames, not exact gesture-age comparisons. Separate equal-age diagnostic runs use a private RAF clock and forced clicks to compare the actual built renderer at identical presentation ages.

The labeled larger anatomy study uses the exact pinned helper and original atlas at original-open/half/full closure, both facings and alpha 1 / 0.5 against a checkerboard. It is a diagnostic browser rendering, not an enlarged production screenshot or edited sprite. The teeth and lower-jaw texture rotate together without the previous mesh study's compressed tooth proportions. I see no conspicuous retained second open jaw, newly separated nose, severed paw/body silhouette or alpha split between the mouth and body. The rough painted red fringe remains stylized flesh/blood rather than convincing saliva simulation. Small hard contour/antialias differences at the moving cut remain a limitation; this inspection is not proof that every pixel of the mouth is anatomically perfect. The six existing pose cuts, repeated silhouettes, standee limbs and crowded mobile formation remain visibly below the owner's craft target.

Equal-age 200/240/280ms comparisons preserve the upper-nose contact position; the lower jaw closes around that unchanged contact. The reopening/recovery cut has the retained baseline appearance by the sampled 370ms frame. This change does not add anticipation duration, resolve all contact overlap with the target, improve hunter defeat or establish a human-tested combat feel.
