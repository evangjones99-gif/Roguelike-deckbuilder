## Gameplay findings

1. Sparse desktop battlefield commands establish a better connection between creature, target and consequence than the baseline's detached rosters. The candidate's new-hunt invitation and optional seed disclosure reduce menu reading. Accepting its contract enters the first fight directly; the baseline requires selecting its sole initial route. Deploying a card added a ready creature without an extra placement choice, and its free arrival-turn command was used through ordinary controls. I did not establish clear first-free-slot highlighting across a full formation.

2. Preview wording supported actual choices in this sample. Widow changed Scour damage; a seven-health Thrall was shown as seven health damage and lethal rather than an uncapped eight. Hex showed five health damage and removal of four block with Widow. The second Hunter fight showed Scour as four health damage and four blocked, then the actual hostile changed from 17 health / 4 block to 13 / 0. Capped finishing commands identified lethal and contract clear. Escape canceled a selected Scour without changing the saved hash in the first candidate fight. These are earned sample observations, not an exhaustive reducer proof; the technical reviewer owns that wider checklist.

3. Hunter pressure created a meaningful priority decision. The Revenant announced six hunter damage ignoring block, so Ward did not answer that hit. Survey disclosed Widow, a persistent spell booster rather than an immediate guaranteed kill. Building bodies while accepting the unblockable hit, then killing that hostile before the Reaver, had visible health consequences. The second fight instead allowed killing Thrall with free commands and a two-mana spell/command lethal line against Reaver, making defensive cancellation unnecessary. Reward and training selections had understandable prospective effects, though later payoff of Grave Tithe/Silence was not tested.

4. REJECTION: the candidate's earned 390 px three-body formations overlap ally identities and health/attack/readiness readouts. In the first Hunter fight, both Cairn Hounds merge at the left cluster. In the second, Fen Stalker and Ash Widow names and numbers overlap; some readiness content sits at or below the field's lower boundary. This occurred through normal draws/deployment, with three allies versus two enemies, well below the maximum six. I could issue actions using accessible DOM identities, but that assistance does not make the displayed formation readable for a player. The baseline's roster text remains separated and readable, while placing allies, arena, enemies, cards and the global preview across long vertical travel. The candidate improves proximity but has not resolved usable density.

5. Final-strike presentation retained the defeated target instead of replacing the battlefield immediately. In candidate 58173, the capture at 63.5 ms after the final click returned shows the 0-HP target; the next capture at 449.0 ms shows its death pose. Reward was observed by 1,678.9 ms after that click returned. Those are sampled observations, not exact animation thresholds. The explicit 'final strike resolving' state gave the strike time to land visually. A rushed 1,000 ms wait elsewhere still reached disabled controls, so command sequences must account for presentation holds. Model deliberation, screenshot time and locator assistance dominate wall time; campaign duration is not a human pacing benchmark.
