# Independent starter-kit and ML evidence review

Decision: accept the reproducibility and negative findings of the frozen exterior research. **Reject promotion of any candidate kit, a changed default starter, or a claim of improved fun.** No production rule, card, policy, save or UI change is authorized by these results alone. Pursuit merits the next controlled prototype; Vigil does not currently support its proposed safer identity. Ash needs a comparator that can evaluate future intent denial before its weakness can be attributed to the deck.

## Independent verification performed

The separately written `replay.ts` streamed the retained gzip into the actual frozen TypeScript engine under Node v24.19.0, using the installed project `tsx`. It verified all **384 initial constructions and 56,645 accepted transitions: 57,029 full records**. Default constructors exactly retain `STARTER_DECK`; candidates differ only by the declared initial deck. Every incoming state was recursively frozen. Every transition has exact legal membership, unchanged input bytes, valid state and JSON save, preserved generation, equal ordinary/event reducer results, exact actual event payloads, and equal JSON-restored reducer results. Every action also matches a separately implemented frozen linear/noncombat selector or the original unchanged heuristic. The extracted learned adapter is byte-exact to the original source section plus its imports, one separator newline and reexports; no training script was invoked.

Every field of all 384 outcomes matches, including initial/final/trajectory hashes, action counts, played cards, health/damage/block/heal metrics, companion deaths, encounter/reward/shop observations and battle summaries. The separate `aggregate.py` reproduced all 288 paired comparisons and all recorded common-world discrepancies exactly. Printed profiles and four posthoc branches were independently checked against actual card definitions, legal reducers and exact events; damage arithmetic also matches.

No new campaigns, tuning, seed selection or policy training were performed. A fresh held-out replication is unnecessary to settle the present question: the negative comparisons and learned-policy limitation are reproducible already. The twelve seeds do not overlap the inspected frozen ML training/test plan, but that is not external proof that every historical experiment used different seeds.

## What the results establish

The 384 episodes contain **12 distinct seed clusters**, repeated across four decks, two engine generations, two policies and two acquisition arms. They are not 384 independent people. Difficulty is Hunter/1. Both combat policies share the same camp-preferring outside policy, skip shops, and either skip rewards or use one frozen reward ranking. No diverse shop/elite/route preparation, novice behavior or other difficulty was tested.

Default wins 96/96; every candidate wins 95/96. None rescues a baseline defeat because none exists. Lower final HP occurs in 58 Pursuit, 58 Vigil and 56 Ash pairs. Pursuit delivers more command damage and fewer turns in these policies, but the loss and health tradeoff prohibit a blanket improvement claim. Vigil repeatedly spends more turns taking more damage; a protection-themed loadout does not establish safer play. Ash shifts damage toward tools while reducing creature durability and fails one matched-world learned/skip run.

Kind1 produces **964** differences among comparable recorded world keys. Deck/draw changes legitimately advance its legacy RNG differently, so its paired outcomes mix kit changes with encounter/offer changes. Kind2 produces **zero** differences among common recorded keys. This is matching common observed keys, not proof that health-dependent acquisition/route decisions or every possible unvisited world event are identical. Kind2 is the stronger primary comparison for a future optional loadout feature. Kind1 remains useful compatibility evidence and must be reported separately.

The generation2-only comparisons do not erase the tradeoffs: Pursuit saves 10.46 actions/1.46 turns but loses 3.81 final HP on average; Vigil adds 3.96 actions/2.17 turns and 7.83 received damage. Ash has 47/48 wins versus 48/48; even its 47 shared-winning pairs have -5.68 HP and +12.60 received damage on average. The extra shared-victory rows in `summary.json` prevent early defeat from being presented as faster completion. Accepted action counts and turns are structural pacing measures, not elapsed player time or boredom scores.

The learned policy casts **zero Silence cards across 24 Ash skip-reward campaigns**, compared with 64 heuristic casts (34 kind1, 30 kind2). Its feature vector evaluates immediate HP/attack/block/energy and terminal changes; it has no explicit prospective reinforcement/intent-denial feature. Learned weights were trained on kind2/default construction, so custom decks and kind1 are distribution changes. The result supports investigating that limitation; it does not establish that Silence, control, or Ash is intrinsically useless, or that correcting the policy will automatically rescue the failed campaign.

All candidates reduce bindings from five to four while retaining twelve total cards. That changes draw density and setup reliability alongside role identity; equal card count and hypothetical shop value are not equal power budgets. No starting gold was charged, and no earned-loadout or actual campaign selection flow was tested.

## Counterplay and next experiments

All four selected branches originate byte-exactly in the retained campaigns and replay legally. Sunder avoids one companion retaliation damage in its chosen branch; Ready makes a previously illegal second command legal and avoids four hunter damage; Aegis reduces selected breath damage 8→3 to the hunter and 29→12 to companions; Silence reduces selected Cantor hunter damage 12→0 while other attackers still cause companion damage 19→7. These branches spend extra energy/cards and were selected after the matrix. They establish mechanisms, not new held-out wins, equal-resource policy superiority, or player enjoyment.

Recommended next work, before any optional starter ships:

1. Predeclare a generation2 cohort and comparison plan for **Pursuit**, retaining this exact first candidate as a baseline. Separate role identity from binding density with an explicitly controlled ablation; retain the default constructor and all current starter bytes. Use new seeds for candidate revisions, record all losses and show both shared-winning and full-cohort metrics. Do not revise this cohort and relabel its rows as untouched.
2. Build a separate **intent-aware comparator** on a distinct training/prototype set, freeze it before a new test cohort, and run the same fixed Ash deck under both old and new policies. Preserve the old learned model and report policy sensitivity. Add the cost of Silence and effects of other attackers; do not simply reward casting the card or silently patch its damage values.
3. Withhold a **Vigil safety claim**. A later defensive candidate needs an explicit setup/tempo hypothesis and measured death, hunter/companion damage and turn tradeoffs against the current default. Adding health or cheaper costs merely to reverse these known rows would require fresh evaluation and could erase its strategic cost.
4. Treat the eventual preparation interface as its own playable gate: exact deck inspection, a plain weakness, default selection, explicit new-run choice and unchanged resumed saves. Observe prospective players choosing plans and explaining their intended first turns. Voluntary human confusion/interesting-choice/replay-intent feedback must remain separate from these model outcomes. No such human session was conducted here.

## Reviewer harness failures and limits

Three reviewer harness failures are retained. `replay-r1.log` failed before execution because the new exterior directory lacked an ESM package marker; `package.json` fixed that environment mismatch. `replay-r2.log` rejected the reviewer's adapter reconstruction because it omitted the author's harmless separator newline; the exact original script is retained as `replay-r1-source.ts`, and the corrected assertion retained all bytes. `profiles-and-branches-r1-wrong-cwd.log` records a command launched from the wrong directory; its newly created reviewer log was moved to this reviewer-owned directory, and the corrected command passed. No frozen author file was modified; the original manifest was checked again. These failures do not constitute game regressions and are not hidden as passing executions.

This is a source, strategic and ML evidence review, not a graphical playtest, audio audition, consumer-platform install test, AAA craft certification, Steam gate, or human-fun consensus. The experimental methodology and explicit negative reporting are strong enough to preserve. The candidate kits are not strong enough to promote from this evidence.
